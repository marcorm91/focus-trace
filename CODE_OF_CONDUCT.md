# FocusTrace Community Code of Conduct

FocusTrace welcomes contributors, users and accessibility practitioners with different backgrounds, experience levels and perspectives.

## Expected behavior

When participating in FocusTrace spaces, please:

- be respectful and specific when discussing code, accessibility findings or standards interpretations;
- critique ideas and implementations rather than people;
- assume good intent while still addressing harmful or inaccessible outcomes clearly;
- make room for people who use assistive technology and for lived experience that may challenge implementation assumptions;
- avoid dismissive language about disability, accessibility requirements or users' needs;
- keep technical disagreement evidence-based and proportionate;
- respect privacy and do not post customer, client, employer or inspected-page data without permission.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, threats, discriminatory language, personal attacks, deliberate misgendering, doxxing, sexual harassment and sustained disruptive behavior are not acceptable.

Publishing vulnerability details or private inspected-page data in a public thread after being asked to use a private security channel may also result in moderation when it creates avoidable risk.

## Scope

This policy applies to project-managed GitHub issues, pull requests, discussions and other official FocusTrace community spaces.

## Reporting conduct problems

Do not escalate a sensitive interpersonal or security issue by posting additional private details publicly. Contact the project maintainer through an appropriate private channel when one is available.

Project maintainers may edit or remove inappropriate content, lock conversations, reject contributions or restrict participation when necessary to protect the community and project.

## Accessibility discussions

Accessibility standards can require careful interpretation. Disagreement is expected. Contributors should distinguish normative requirements, informative guidance, implementation experience and personal preference so discussions remain useful and technically honest.
